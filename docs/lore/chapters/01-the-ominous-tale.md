## Part 1: A Letter From the Sky

A raven drops out of a clear sky and lands on your pack as if it owned it. Around its leg is a letter sealed in
white-gold wax. Wrapped inside the letter is a small round mirror.

> "To whoever is brave enough to open this. My name is Maren Vell. I keep the archive of the Wayfarers' Lodge, in
> Skyhearth, which you have probably never heard of. That's all right. Few have. Hold the mirror up and say my name. I
> would rather explain in person. Or nearly in person."

The glass clouds, clears, and then she is standing beside you. She is taller than anyone you know, and a little
see-through, with a faint gold in her eyes. This is Maren's Image, and the mirror is Skyhearth's Mirror: it lets her
reach you wherever you are.

She is kind and brisk. The old gate stones are cracking, she says, and the Lodge needs someone to walk them. First she
has to know that you can look after yourself. The letter in your hand is more than a letter: it is a trial, and she
would like you to read it somewhere with room to move.

You find open ground and read the scroll aloud. Stones rise out of the earth around you in a low ring. Grass and flowers
vanish under them, and a small arena stands where you were. Maren's Image waits at one side of it. In the middle stands
a straw dummy with a zombie's head and dyed leather armor, which Maren insists is "traditional".

> "The Lodge will imbue you with magic for this trial, enabling you to use three spells, one from each path a
> {className} can specialize in. Try each of them on the dummy. It won't mind. It has been hit by folks way stronger
> than either of us."

The three spells feel strange in your hands, then less strange. When the dummy has had all three, Maren nods, and the
dummy crumbles into straw.

> "Good. Now something that hits back."

Three trial zombies climb out of the ground inside the ring. The sun is high, and they do not burn: the scroll called
them, and the scroll does not care what time it is. If they get the better of you, lightning cracks down and the trial's
dead fall still. Maren only says:

> "Not quite. Again. The trial doesn't mind waiting, and neither do I. Yet."

When the last one falls, you feel it: a lift, as if you had grown an inch inside.

> "There it is. Strength comes quickly to you, {className}, and you'll want to spend it well."

She tells you where it will serve you best, says goodbye to the arena, and the stones sink back into the earth.

Later, when you call her again, she has time to talk properly ("The More You Know"). The Lodge, she explains, is an old
order of the Ithari, her people: road-wardens who walk the gates and check on the keepers. Long ago the Ithari's makers,
the Gatewrights, raised five realms around the Overworld like a sea wall against the Void, and shut each realm's first
gate behind a seal. A keeper holds every seal, and keepers are relieved now and then, like a changing of the guard. The
oldest of them are tiring now, and their seals are cracking.

Then why ask you, a stranger from the Overworld?

> "Because the road doesn't work on us. My people pass every gate, and the trials stand aside for us, so the road never
> changes us. You grow with every trial you pass. And when one of you falls, the world pulls you home. We know every old
> road. We lack a body that grows stronger."

From then on the Lodge calls you a Wayfarer.

**In the game**

- **Quest 1, the raven:** the first quest delivers Skyhearth's Mirror and Maren's letter, which is **Maren's Trial
  Scroll** (decided: the letter is the scroll, so the raven's one delivery is the only thing that crosses from
  Skyhearth).
- **Quest 2, Maren's Trial** (RLM-397):
  - the arena (Matěj builds it with WorldEdit, RLM-393) and the MmmMmmMmmMmm dummy;
  - one spell per specialization on R, T and F;
  - three trial zombies that don't burn in daylight;
  - on a fall: half health, lightning, a retry that heals;
  - a level-up, and the stat hint on the character sheet;
  - the reward is a basic Backpack.
- **Quest 3, "The More You Know":** call Maren's Image and talk; the reward is the backpack's Crafting Upgrade. This is
  where beat 2 ("Why It Has to Be You") lives.

## Part 2: The Open Crypts

The cracks did not stay in the seals. Each one shook the Overworld too, and old crypts that had been shut for centuries
split open: cellars under abandoned Wayfarer outposts, tombs under the hills. What was buried in them walks again.

> "Those tombs were shut for a reason. Mostly for the neighbors' sake."

First you need a way to find them, and Maren has one:

> "Wayfarers lose those compasses the way other people lose gloves. Open any old chest out there, and one will turn up."

She is right. The first old chest you open, in a ruin, a mine or a tomb, holds a Wayfarer's Compass. Its needle doesn't
point north. It points at old places.

Maren sends you to the nearest crypts: a catacomb where the dead shuffle in rows, a fortress of old bones where
skeletons keep a watch nobody ordered, a cave where the spiders have had the place to themselves for far too long.

Every crypt holds a page from its outpost's last journal. A Wayfarer wrote them, long ago, about small things: how to
read a ruin, how to mend a boot, how to keep a fire going in the rain. Each page teaches you one thing worth knowing.
And every page mentions, sooner or later, "the hall over the breach", the way people mention home.

The dead, though, are behaving oddly. In a larger dungeon past the crypts, you find them standing in lines, bones
stacked neatly by the door, as if someone had been tidying. Someone has.

Whoever it is has also been gathering the risen dead into an old watchtower, the Tower of the Undead, and sending them
against the villages. You climb it floor by floor, through the dead on every landing, all the way to the top. Whoever
gathered them isn't there. Only his red banners hang on the walls.

The banners lead you underground, into an illager hideout. In the room at its heart, between caged allays and one very
confused zombie villager, you find him: an evoker. He found the open crypts before you did. He was no mastermind. He was
paid: in Gatewright brick, pried out of the old ruins by the illagers' chief. You put an end to him, and the last thing
he says is about the chief, and "the hall with five doors".

> "Someone is paying in Gatewright brick? Then someone is taking the ruins apart. I don't like that at all."

His last command still stands, though. His dead have gone to ground in the oldest maze in the land, under the roots of
an ancient dark oak, among every kind of foe the dark has gathered there over the years. You go in after them. It is the
hardest fight yet, all the way to the maze's heart, where its tunnels meet. When it is over, the dead in this part of
the world stay dead. On the heart's wall is a Wayfarer mural: a round hall, five great doors in a ring.

> "That's the Lodge's hall. The Wayfarers drew it everywhere they went, so none of them would forget the way home."

**In the game** (decided 24 October; every count is checked in a test world)

1.  **The compass:** the first loot container a player opens outside a village holds the Wayfarer's Compass (RLM-319),
    and Lootr gives every player their own. A recipe makes another one: a compass in the middle, rotten flesh above, a
    bone on the left, a spider eye on the right, an ender pearl below, and chiseled stone bricks in the corners. No
    quest shop resale.
2.  **Every dungeon quest is "visit" plus one goal inside that structure,** and nothing is added to the structures
    (RLM-320):
    - YUNG's Catacombs: collect 10 Zombie Heads (the quest checks them and doesn't take them);
    - YUNG's Fortress of the Undead: collect 10 Skeleton Skulls;
    - YUNG's Spider Cave: collect 20 Cobwebs (bring shears);
    - Dungeons Enhanced's Large Dungeon: lay 10 of its dead to rest (kills inside it);
    - Dungeons Enhanced's Tower of the Undead: reach its top floor;
    - Dungeons and Taverns' Illager Hideout: defeat the evoker in its heart room, the one evoker every hideout holds;
    - Dungeons Enhanced's Monster Maze: reach its heart and defeat 20 monsters inside.
3.  Each crypt quest may give a read-me page.
4.  **Clues are story only,** never a map item: the quest journal, the compass and Xaero's map lead the player.

## Part 3: The Raiders

With the dead quiet, the living trouble shows. Pillagers squat in the cracked Gatewright ruins, loot whatever the cracks
have opened, and raid the villages. Bad Omen is their chief's mark. Strike down one of his captains and drink from the
bottle he carries, and the mark is yours; carry it into a village, and the raid follows you.

Where did they come from? Maren knows, and she isn't proud of it.

> "We left them. Long ago, people of your kind worked at the Lodge's hall: porters, cooks, the ones who swept the gates.
> When the war came and the Wayfarers went up to Skyhearth, they were forced to stay, fight and hide. They became angry
> and bitter. Seven hundred years have passed since then. The war ended, and things got better. Now, though, they have
> made up their minds that the gates were promised to them, and that we took the promise with us."

They are the illagers. Their banners still copy an old Lodge mark, badly.

The damage is plain to see. Villages are raided wherever the mark is carried, crops are burnt, iron golems are smashed.
Worse, every ruin you pass is missing bricks: the illagers pry the Gatewright stones loose and carry them off. The
villagers call their leader only "the Ominous Chief". They say he never shows the same face twice.

When a village under Bad Omen sends for you, you hold the line through every wave. Afterwards the villagers tell you
what they overheard: the raiders spoke of "the chief's tower", and of "the hall with five doors".

Then there is the castle. The illagers took a whole castle from its people, the Bastille, and kept the people. The
villagers who once lived there are kept in cages in the courtyard, let out only to work. You fight your way through the
castle until its garrison is broken. The cages open, and the castle fills with its people again: villagers in every hall
and doorway, iron golems back at the gates.

> "They kept the cooks. Raiders always keep the cooks."

**In the game**

1.  **The illager quests**, main path in this order:
    1.  **a pillager outpost** (vanilla), before the raids: visit it and get an Ominous Bottle, which only a pillager
        captain drops; about 1 in 17 pillagers there is a captain, and every patrol has one;
    2.  **the raid quests:** first wave to Nth wave, then "Defend a Village"; Bad Omen repeats up to about 3 times
        (RLM-320). Raids stay vanilla, by day or by night;
    3.  Illager Invasion's **Illager Fort**: visit it and defeat 15 illagers inside;
    4.  the **Bastille**.
2.  **The Bastille liberation:** Matěj's cleared rule and the villagers returning (RLM-398), with its cinematic later
    (RLM-396).
    - A plain liberation, with no illusion: the cages are fine, since the "no cages before chapter 8" rule guards only
      chapter 8's twist.
    - It Takes a Pillage has no mage illager, so the rule's "a mage killed" needs a substitute (see RLM-398).

## Part 4: The Hall Over the Breach

The compass leads you there at last. The Lodge's great hall stands over the breach, where the world's edge once crumbled
deepest. It is very old, and very quiet. Five great shut gates stand in a ring inside it, each the Overworld side of one
seal, a glowing signet set into each of their sealstones. All five are cracked.

Behind them is the vault. Its door lies in pieces. The vault is empty. Maren's voice goes very quiet.

> "They took it. The signet for the stone realm. The Gatewrights knew the road would never change their own people, so
> they left a way for someone it would. One signet, here, on this side of the Wall, and behind every seal a trial that
> guards the next one. They never knew who would come, or when. They only made sure that whoever came would earn every
> step. It has waited here since the Wall was raised. For you, I think. And someone else reached it first."

Then you notice the fresh chisel marks on all five gates. The illagers tried to pry the signets out of the sealstones
themselves. The seals held, as seals do, but every blow widened a crack.

> "Five thousand years of holding, and someone brought a chisel."

For a while she doesn't say anything. Then the mirror shows you something it never has before: a younger Maren, faint as
a memory, walking into the hall the way she did before the Lodge moved to Skyhearth. She walks the ring of gates,
slowly, and names each one as she passes. "Stone. The Twins, sun and ice. The Deep One. The Mire, which nobody liked."
At the stone gate, the one the chisels hit hardest, she lays her hand on the stone, and the memory fades.

**In the game**

- **The hall:** a unique custom structure, one per world, found with the Wayfarer's Compass. Matěj builds it with
  WorldEdit (RLM-393), and it is imported, with the five cracked seal gates as our own blocks.
- **A mirror call** for the scene: the vault and the chisel marks.
- **Maren's memory** is a walk-and-talk scene (M2, RLM-408): her younger self walks a path through the hall with timed
  lines, while the camera stays with the player. A camera can come later with RLM-396.

## Part 5: The Gate

Maren has the Gatewrights' gate plan from the Lodge's archive, and she reads it to you like a recipe.

The gate is made of the Overworld's own stone: bricks of eight kinds of stone bound around an ender pearl make
Gatewright Bricks. A frame of them stands around one keystone, the Gatewright Sealstone. You build it, and it is the
first gate a Wayfarer has raised in an age.

It stays dark. A gate needs a signet, and the signet for the stone realm is in the chief's hands.

> "So that's what he wanted the bricks for. He's been building a gate of his own."

**In the game**

- **The gate blocks:** Gatewright Bricks and the Gatewright Sealstone, crafted directly from the bricks and an ender
  pearl (RLM-394).
- **The quest chain:** gather the bricks, craft, raise the frame. The gate is dark because the chief has the signet.

## Part 6: The Ominous Chief

The Illusioner, chief of the illagers, holds a tower in the dark forest. What he wants lies beyond the first gate: the
Gatewrights' storehouse, full of metal no smith can buy. With it his people would never need to raid for steel again.
That is why he took the signet, and why his people pried the bricks: he raised a gate of his own.

It never opened. A signet alone opens nothing, because the seal knows a traveler by their deeds. He held the signet for
months and never got through. In the end he tried to force the seal with it, and the old stone signet broke in his
hands. A pattern is whole in every piece of it, though. He kept the largest piece and shared the others with the chiefs
of his other towers, in case one of them found the trick he couldn't.

You fight your way up his tower, floor by floor. At the top stands the gate he built, dark, its bricks pried from a
hundred ruins. He hides among his own mirror images, but Skyhearth's Mirror shows you which one is real. When he falls,
his last illusion fades with a sneer:

> "Then let it crack on its own."

**In the game**

- **The tower** (decided 24 October): Illager Invasion's **Illusioner Tower**, a multi-floor tower with an Illusioner
  fight at the top (noted on RLM-321). The Illusioner is level 8 with our own AI and phases, and no Blindness. His boss
  bar reads "Illusioner, the Ominous Chief".
- **Optional extra goal:** Illusionary Dust, Illager Invasion's own item, from the tower's stair chests (RLM-320).
- **Skyhearth's Mirror** reveals the real Illusioner among his images, a fight mechanic.
- **Several towers per world** match the shared pieces: every Illusioner carries one (RLM-395's "always a source").

## Part 7: The Signet of the Undervault

The Illusioner drops his piece of the old signet. You call Maren's Image, and she turns it over in her see-through
hands.

> "That's it. Broken, but a pattern is whole in every piece of it. Bind it with the finest gems the Overworld has and a
> little of the stone it opens, and the seal will know it. It will know you, too: you're the one who earned it."

You bind the shard with diamonds and stone, and you have the Signet of the Undervault. Maren only looks at it; it is
yours to keep. As you come near the sealstone with it in your hand, the stone slowly brightens, as if it knew you. When
you hold the signet to it, the gate lights.

The bond on your strength loosens. The seals have weighed you, and they let you grow.

> "Now the hard part. The stone realm has been shut for a very long time. Wipe your boots."

**In the game**

- **The drop:** the Illusioner drops the Stone Signet Shard, his piece of the old signet, at 100 % (decided 24 October).
- **The craft** (decided 24 October, RLM-395): the shard in the middle, 4 diamonds on the sides and 4 stone in the
  corners make the Signet of the Undervault. Every signet follows the same rule: its shard in the middle, 4 of the
  previous realm's ore or gem on the sides, and 4 of the new realm's theme block in the corners (the Signet of the
  Sunwastes: 4 netherite scraps and 4 sand). Maren's Image only confirms it, and nothing is handed over or sent.
- **The sealstone wakes** (RLM-406): the Gatewright Sealstone lights up smoothly while a player holding a signet stands
  near it.
- **The chapter's end:** the final boss quest grants chapter 1's advancement, so the level cap rises.
- **The Lodge Merchant** (RLM-407), the quest journal's shop tab: it sells the Signet of the Undervault for 50 copper,
  up to 3 times, unlocked only after the Illusioner quest (RLM-395). Its sell quests buy mob loot for coins at low
  prices, without limit: 64 Rotten Flesh for 10 copper, a Zombie Head for 5 copper.

## Optional quests

Not part of the main story, loosely tied to it, and not every structure needs one:

- **"More Undeads?":** Dungeons and Taverns' Undead Crypt (decided 24 October), an undead dungeon no other quest uses.
- **"Even MORE Undeads?":** Dungeons Enhanced's Deep Crypt was decided on 24 October, but the realm design places it in
  Undervault (RLM-316), so it moves to chapter 2's optional quests. **Decided (25 October), a correction after the
  approval:** Dungeons and Taverns' Stray Fort instead, a fort on snowy plains and in snowy taigas where strays (frozen
  skeletons) keep spawning, with a catacomb beneath. No other quest uses it. The quest: visit it and defeat 15 strays
  inside.
- **Non-undead Overworld structures** (decided 24 October), each rewarding XP, a few items and some coins:
  - Battle Towers (floors of spawners and a golem at the top);
  - Dungeons Enhanced's Jungle Monument and Pirate Ship;
  - Illager Invasion's Labyrinth, as an optional illager dungeon;
  - Dungeons and Taverns' Illager Manor, a huge manor with several evokers (decided 24 October).
- **Kept out:** the Ancient City and other higher-level structures, which wait for their own decision.

## In M1

M1 has no NPCs, illager structures or Illusioner, so its quest book tells a short version with Maren's lines as letters:

1.  her letter;
2.  "Why It Has to Be You";
3.  the first levels and spells;
4.  the gate (Part 5);
5.  the last quest, which gives the Signet of the Undervault as its reward, as it does today, with no story for where it
    came from.

When M2 brings the rest, Parts 1 to 7 replace it.

## Decisions

1.  **Decided (24 October): the letter is Maren's Trial Scroll,** so the raven's one delivery (the mirror and the
    letter) is the only thing that crosses from Skyhearth.
2.  **Decided (24 October): the Wayfarer's Compass** comes from the first loot container a player opens outside a
    village, in any structure, and a recipe makes another (Part 2).
3.  **Decided (24 October): the structures,** in order: YUNG's Catacombs, Fortress of the Undead and Spider Cave;
    Dungeons Enhanced's Large Dungeon and Tower of the Undead; Dungeons and Taverns' Illager Hideout; Dungeons
    Enhanced's Monster Maze; then a pillager outpost, the raids, Illager Invasion's Illager Fort, the Bastille, and
    Illager Invasion's Illusioner Tower.
4.  **Decided: the Illusioner's drop,** the Stone Signet Shard, a piece of the old stone signet he broke trying to force
    the seal. Every Illusioner carries a piece, which makes him the repeatable source.
5.  **Decided: why the illagers pried Gatewright bricks:** to raise their own gate, which the stolen signet couldn't
    open. This ties Parts 3, 5 and 6 together.
6.  **Decided: the Bastille is a plain liberation:** villagers in cages, freed when the castle is cleared.
7.  **Decided: the mirror scenes.** Maren's Image appears for five of them (the raven, the trial, "The More You Know",
    the hall, the shard), and the hall adds Maren's memory (RLM-408). Her other lines here are quest journal text in her
    voice.
8.  **Decided (24 October): "Illusioner, the Ominous Chief".** With the title there is no article in front ("Fight
    Illusioner, the Ominous Chief in his tower"); without it, "the Illusioner" ("Fight the Illusioner in his tower").
9.  **Decided (24 October):** the quest shop sells the Signet of the Undervault for 50 copper, up to 3 times, and stays
    locked until the Illusioner quest is done (RLM-395, RLM-407).
10. **Decided (24 October): the optional undead dungeons** are Dungeons and Taverns' Undead Crypt and a second one, the
    Illager Manor is an optional quest too, and the chief "has been building a gate of his own". The second dungeon was
    Dungeons Enhanced's Deep Crypt, which belongs to Undervault; since 25 October it is Dungeons and Taverns' Stray Fort
    (Optional quests).
