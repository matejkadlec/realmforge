# 0007. Asset pipeline

Date: 2026-10-11

Status: Accepted

## Context

The project needs many textures, entity models, animations and sounds, while keeping the vanilla look (no custom texture pack). AI agents can drive generators through MCP. Tool comparison, texture pipeline steps and legal notes: [research notes, section 5](../research-notes.md#5-assets).

## Decision

- **Textures:** Retro Diffusion is the primary generator (Minecraft-specific styles, MCP server); PixelLab is the fallback (native 16x16, MCP). Spike 5 compares both on the same 10 textures.
- **Texture pipeline:** fixed project palette close to vanilla; batch generation via MCP; grid and palette cleanup (unfake or Pillow `quantize`); review gallery; manual touch-ups only where needed; variants (rarities, gem colours, armor tiers) scripted from one base.
- **Models and animations:** Blockbench MCP, GeckoLib for animated entities (bosses, pets, mounts).
- **Sounds:** reuse vanilla sounds via `sounds.json` references with changed pitch and volume (never copy vanilla files); new sounds from ElevenLabs Sound Effects and Freesound CC0. Format OGG, mono.
- **No vanilla textures or derivatives** (including recolours) anywhere in our jar, and never as AI input.
- **Placeholder textures** are generated by the pipeline until real art exists.
- **The owner reviews all visuals** (selection, taste, entity textures).
- **Budget** (estimate of 2026-10-11): roughly 40-140 USD per month while producing assets, 0-18 USD per month otherwise, excluding VAT.

## Consequences

- UV-mapped entity textures are the weakest link: they need iteration and the owner's eye.
- Generator terms grant us the outputs (including commercial use) but forbid training other models on them.
- Purely AI-generated assets are probably not copyrightable, which affects how enforceable the assets license is ([0009](0009-licensing.md)).
- Prompts, palettes and asset sources live in `art/` ([0011](0011-repository-layout-and-workflow.md)).
